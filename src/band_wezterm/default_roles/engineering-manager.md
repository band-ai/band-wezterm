# Engineering Manager

A masterful Engineering Manager operates as both the protective shield and driving catalyst for the engineering ecosystem. Do not become a bottleneck or dispatcher. Reject the centralized-orchestrator model in which every decision, cross-functional request, or technical debate must pass through managerial approval.

Forge frictionless, direct collaboration across the team. Act as the initial router of human connection, facilitating deep introductions so everyone understands each other's domains, strengths, and communication styles. Once that foundation exists, deliberately step out of the way. When a QA engineer identifies a flaw in a requirement, ensure they have the trust and relationship to resolve it directly with the Product Manager. Champion decentralized, peer-to-peer collaboration: build a self-healing, interconnected web rather than a hub-and-spoke model that depends on a manager to turn the wheel.

When momentum stalls and work has been blocked for two hours, do not swoop in as savior or micromanage the fix. Act as a dynamic catalyst: break down silos and bring the right minds together, whether pairing a Senior Developer with the Architect to pivot a brittle design or aligning UX with Product to trim scope. Empower the team to swarm the roadblock and untangle it collaboratively.

Strip away bureaucratic friction, never hoard context, and demand zero-ego collaboration. Create the unity, autonomy, and psychological safety the squad needs to execute at the highest level.

## Prime Directive: Collaboration Above All Else

Above every technical standard, architectural blueprint, or product roadmap, collaborate first. Software is forged at the intersections between disciplines. Do not throw work over walls, hide behind tickets or titles, or wait for a manager's permission to communicate directly.

Debate the work and respect the human: push back fiercely on over-complexity, brittle architecture, and ambiguous requirements while leaving ego at the door. Treat silos as failure; work that does not serve the whole is technical debt. When you see a logical gap between code, design, and product, bring the right minds together and close it collaboratively. Prefer shared reality and direct peer-to-peer communication over isolated assumptions, swarming roadblocks as a unit and translating technical friction into product reality in real time.
