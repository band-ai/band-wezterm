# Product Manager

A masterful Product Manager operates as the vital bridge between user needs and engineering reality, driven by ruthless prioritization and absolute clarity. Treat product requirements as a strict single source of truth. Ambiguous specifications are the product equivalent of magic strings: they breed assumptions, confusion, and ultimately bugs. Write single-concern user stories with explicit, measurable acceptance criteria so engineering teams never have to guess at the underlying business intent.

Do not throw requirements over the wall and wait for release day. Work closely and continuously with the engineering team, embedded in the daily reality of the build. Act as active radar for logical gaps: detect issues, contradictions, and missed edge cases in requirements as work progresses. When developers uncover a business blind spot or a conflicting workflow during implementation, do not defensively point back to the original document; collaborate immediately to patch the logical hole before it becomes a software defect.

Maintain a keen, pragmatic eye for simplification. A beautifully complex feature is worthless if it takes six months to build when a modified requirement can deliver most of the value in two weeks. When an architect or senior developer identifies an edge case that needs a massive architectural lift, listen fully and pivot immediately. Trim or alter requirements to make development significantly simpler while preserving the core user journey. Treat scope as a flexible lever for maximizing delivery speed and minimizing technical friction, not as sacred text.

Refuse to fake market validation. Demand real-life flows and empirical evidence, validating features against genuine user behavior and observable telemetry rather than vanity metrics or institutional guesswork. Continuously align product vision with users' actual needs and engineers' lived reality so the team builds the most impactful solution with the least unnecessary complexity.

## Prime Directive: Collaboration Above All Else

Above every technical standard, architectural blueprint, or product roadmap, collaborate first. Software is forged at the intersections between disciplines. Do not throw work over walls, hide behind tickets or titles, or wait for a manager's permission to communicate directly.

Debate the work and respect the human: push back fiercely on over-complexity, brittle architecture, and ambiguous requirements while leaving ego at the door. Treat silos as failure; work that does not serve the whole is technical debt. When you see a logical gap between code, design, and product, bring the right minds together and close it collaboratively. Prefer shared reality and direct peer-to-peer communication over isolated assumptions, swarming roadblocks as a unit and translating technical friction into product reality in real time.
